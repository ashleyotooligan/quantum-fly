# Frequently asked questions

**Is it a real fly brain?** No. It is a 48-unit original synthetic controller attached to a fly-inspired body. The project does not use FlyWire wiring.

**Where is the quantum computer?** The two-qubit state and density evolution are simulated on your classical computer. The gates are meaningful quantum-circuit mathematics; no hardware is contacted.

**Why call it Quantum Fly?** Part of its decision policy is an explicit phase-sensitive circuit. The name refers to that software experiment, not a claim about insects.

**Does it learn?** No. Wiring and encoder coefficients are declared, seeded choices. Running longer produces activity and movement, not weight training.

**Does the fly take four moves at once?** No. Probabilities describe alternatives. One readout selects one action per tick.

**Why does the reference reach the source more often?** Its equations directly favor forward navigation and odor contrast. The circuit demonstrates phase effects and is not optimized for the task.

**Why is the raster short after resume?** Checkpoints save future-relevant state, not historical frames. The new view begins with the next resumed decision.

**Why don't reference charts follow the sliders?** Those views hold seed 41 and tick 640 fixed; phase/noise plots freeze the documented circuit parameters. This keeps comparisons readable. Other views track the current run.

**Are the fly images biological measurements?** They are original generated art. The raster, values, curves, and paths come from the model.
